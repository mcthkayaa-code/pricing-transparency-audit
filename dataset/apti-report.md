# AI Pricing Transparency Index (APTI), a determinability index — computed results

Generated by `tools/score_apti.py` from `dataset/coded-values.csv`. Every figure here is
reproducible from `dataset/apti-scores.csv`, which carries each product's twenty item
scores. The instrument is pre-registered in `protocol-v1.md` section 8.3 and was fixed
before any datum existed; this tool implements it and changes nothing.

**The index scores determinability, not generosity.** A vendor stating "no refunds under
any circumstances" earns the same six points on D3 as one stating a 30-day unconditional
refund. Both readers know what they are buying, and knowing is the whole of what the
index measures.

> **These scores are provisional.** Three adjudications and a retrieval sweep were open
> when this ran and may change a handful of coded values. The tool is the durable
> deliverable: re-running it is one command and costs nothing.

## 4. How much of each score rests on `unknown`

337 of 2812 (12.0%) coded values in the corpus are `unknown`. They split three ways, and the three add back to 337: **240** sit on active products, on a variable an index item reads, and cost those products points; **75** sit on active products on a variable no index item reads, and cost nothing; **22** sit on the 3 discontinued products, which carry no score at all under rule D8. Collapsed from variables to items under rule G5, active products carry 184 of 1316 (14.0%) applicable items in an `unknown` state.

Rule G1 is the study's central stance: an `unknown` scores zero and stays in the
denominator, because an undisclosed term is the buyer's burden and not a missing
observation. So an unknown is not noise around a score — it is part of the score, and a
reader is entitled to see how much of any product's number is built from them.

- **Full frame** — item-level `unknown` count per product (rule G5): median **2.0**, IQR 1.0 to 3.0 (width 2.0), min 0.0, max 13.0; mean 2.5 beside the median, n = 73
- **Full frame** — `determinability_rate` per product (rule G0, item level): median **0.82**, IQR 0.75 to 0.89 (width 0.14), min 0.32, max 1.0; mean 0.81 beside the median, n = 73
- **Paid submissions removed** — item-level `unknown` count per product (rule G5): median **2.0**, IQR 1.0 to 3.0 (width 2.0), min 0.0, max 13.0; mean 2.5 beside the median, n = 72
- **Paid submissions removed** — `determinability_rate` per product (rule G0, item level): median **0.82**, IQR 0.75 to 0.89 (width 0.14), min 0.32, max 1.0; mean 0.81 beside the median, n = 72

A score computed over fewer determinate items is not comparable to one computed over
more, which is why rule G0 defines determinacy at the item level and why every score in
section 8 prints its `determinability_rate` and its determinate-of-applicable counts
beside it. The two figures answer different questions: the index asks how many of the
available points the documents earn, and the determinability rate asks how many of the
questions the documents answer at all.

## 6. Independent hand computation against this tool

A tool that reads less than it claims produces a number nobody can distinguish from a
result. That has happened six times in this study and three of those numbers were
reassuring. So five products were scored **by hand**, item by item, straight from the
protocol text and the record, without this tool, and then compared against it. The four
picked deliberately plus one: a high scorer, a low scorer, one carrying several `unknown`
values, and two carrying `not_applicable` inside a multi-variable item — one where the
matrix removes the item and one where rule G4 keeps it at full points.

- canva: all figures agree
- fotor: all figures agree
- google-veo: all figures agree
- ismybrandinai: all figures agree
- sapling: all figures agree

**They disagreed on one of the five, and the hand pass was the wrong one.** The hand pass
scored `sapling` at 89.5 by reading item F1's variable `usage_cap_quantified` as
`not_applicable` and removing the item. All three storage shapes carry
`some_quantified`, and the adjudicated record says so in words. The hand pass had carried
the value across from `ismybrandinai`, scored immediately before, which does carry
`not_applicable` there. Corrected by hand from the record, F1 scores 3 of 6, earned
becomes 54 of 63, and the index is **85.7** — which is what the tool had. The hand error
ran 3.8 points **high**: the flattering direction, and the one this study is most
suspicious of. It is recorded here rather than reconciled away.

The five hand figures are frozen inside `tools/score_apti.py` as `HAND_VERIFIED` and
re-checked on every run, so a later change to a coded value on one of these products is
reported as a divergence instead of drifting quietly.

## 7. Protocol coverage

Every coded value was looked up in an explicit table transcribed from section 8.3. The
tool has no default branch: a value with no defined outcome stops the run.

- Coded values looked up in a section 8.3 table: 1460 item evaluations over 73 active products. Combinations with no defined outcome: **0**. The run would have stopped and written nothing if there were any.
- Unreachable value pairs that nonetheless reached the dataset: **0**. The A3 matrix's daggered cells, the B1 matrix's two not-reachable rows and item B3's named impossible pair were all checked on every product.
- Latent ambiguity, no effect on this wave: rule G0's closing bullet makes `no_public_price` on item A1 determinate — the vendor has documented a decision to withhold the price — while the same value scores 0 for determinability of the amount. A product could therefore print a high `determinability_rate` beside a zeroed A1. No product in this corpus carries `no_public_price`, so nothing here turns on it, but a later wave with a sales-gated vendor will need the reading stated.
- Interpretation recorded, not invented: sensitivity analysis S2 removes items whose **item-level** value is `unknown` under rule G5, which is the only item-level definition of `unknown` the protocol gives and the one `unknown_count` uses. A multi-variable item scoring above zero with one `unknown` sub-variable is therefore not removed, matching G5's third bullet.
