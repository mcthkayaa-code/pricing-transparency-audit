Model & agent prices are subject to change. We update pricing as underlying models change; current
prices are always shown in the app before you confirm a generation.

How the range works
Each model shows a credits range spanning its cheapest tier (e.g. lowest resolution, no audio,
shortest duration) to its most expensive tier (e.g. highest resolution, with audio, longest
duration). You'll see the exact cost for your chosen configuration before you confirm.

Tips for controlling video spend
Pick the lowest resolution that matches your output need — 720p is fine for most social formats.
Shorter clips cost proportionally fewer credits...

=== CODING NOTES ===
- credit_to_output_rate_published: rates ARE published for the principal output (video), across
  every model the platform offers, satisfying the "published" test — but every single model's rate
  is stated ONLY as a range (min-max spanning its resolution/duration/audio tiers), with no formula
  resolving which exact figure applies to a given configuration beyond "you'll see the exact cost
  ...in the app before you confirm" (i.e. the exact figure lives behind product use, not in a
  document). This matches the codebook's `partial` definition verbatim: "Rates are published for
  the principal output ... or only as a range."
- cost_per_output_computable: computing a single "cost per video minute" figure for the entry tier
  requires first choosing WHICH of the 60+ models a "typical" buyer would use — the range spans
  roughly 0.02 to 67+ credits per generation across the full roster, so no single model can be
  picked without an assumption about typical use. Per codebook rule 7 ("If it needs an assumption
  about how a typical buyer uses the product, code no"), this is coded `no`, not `partial` — the
  ambiguity here is not "one output, one range" (the codebook's worked examples) but "one output
  category standing in for 60+ separately-priced sub-products", which is a materially different
  and more severe disclosure gap than a single bounded range.
